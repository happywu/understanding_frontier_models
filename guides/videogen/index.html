The Evolution: Why Now?

2020-2022: U-Net Diffusion Era

DDPM, LDM (Stable Diffusion) proved latent diffusion works for images. U-Net backbone, discrete noise schedules, hundreds of sampling steps.

2023: DiT + Flow Matching

DiT paper (Peebles & Xie) replaced U-Net with ViT. Flow matching (Lipman et al.) introduced straight ODE paths. These two innovations converge.

2024: Video Generation Explosion

Sora, CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Movie Gen, Stable Video Diffusion. 3D VAEs, spacetime patches, 13B+ parameter models. Flow matching becomes dominant.

2025: Scale + Alignment

Veo 3 (joint audio-video), Wan 2.1 (#1 VBench), Goku, GRPO/DPO for video alignment. 30B-49B parameter frontier. RL-based fine-tuning matures.

Step by Step

1

Encode Text

The text prompt is encoded into a sequence of embedding vectors using a pre-trained text encoder (T5-XXL, CLIP, or an MLLM). These embeddings capture the semantic meaning of the prompt and will guide the generation process.

Key insight: Different encoders have complementary strengths. CLIP provides aligned image-text embeddings (good for global semantics), while T5 provides rich token-level representations (good for compositional details). Many models use both.

2

Denoise with DiT

Starting from pure Gaussian noise in the latent space, the Diffusion Transformer iteratively refines the latent representation. At each step, it predicts a velocity field (flow matching) or noise (DDPM) conditioned on the text embeddings and current timestep.

Key insight: The DiT operates on spacetime patches — 3D tokens that span both space and time. This allows a single transformer to jointly model spatial composition and temporal dynamics.

3

Decode Latent to Pixels

The clean latent z1 is decoded back to pixel space by the VAE decoder. This involves upsampling in both spatial and temporal dimensions using transposed 3D convolutions.

Key insight: The VAE was pre-trained separately to achieve high-fidelity reconstruction. A 4×8×8 compression ratio means the DiT only needs to operate on a representation that is 256× smaller than the original video.

Why Latent Space?

📈

Computational Cost Comparison

For a 5-second, 720p, 24fps video:

Pixel space: 120 frames × 1280 × 720 × 3 = 332M values Latent space: 30 × 160 × 90 × 16 = 6.9M values Ratio: ~48× fewer values to denoise

Self-attention in the transformer scales as O(n²). Reducing the sequence length by 48× reduces attention cost by ~2,300×. This is why all frontier video models operate in latent space.

Classifier-Free Guidance

At inference time, CFG amplifies the effect of text conditioning. The model is run twice per step — once with the text prompt, once without — and the outputs are combined:

The guidance scale w (typically 5–15 for video) controls how strongly the output follows the prompt. Higher values produce more faithful but less diverse outputs.

⚠

CFG Doubles Compute

Because CFG requires two forward passes per step, it doubles inference cost. Recent innovations like CFG-Zero* (2025) optimize the guidance scaling and zero out early ODE steps where velocity estimation is most inaccurate, improving quality without extra cost.

3D VAE Tokenization

Before the diffusion model can work its magic, raw video must be compressed into a compact latent representation. This is the job of the 3D Variational Autoencoder.

Why Compress?

A single second of 720p video contains ~71 million pixel values. Running diffusion directly in pixel space would require attention over millions of tokens — computationally impossible. The VAE compresses this by 256× spatially (4× temporal, 8× in each spatial dimension), making diffusion tractable.

From 2D to 3D: The Evolution

Early latent diffusion (Stable Diffusion, SDXL) used a 2D spatial VAE: each frame is independently encoded into a 2D latent, then the model generates frame-by-frame. This works for images but fails for video — there is no temporal compression, adjacent frames have no latent-level coherence, and the result is flickering, temporally inconsistent output.

2D Spatial VAE

Compresses each frame independently: H×W×3 → H/8×W/8×4. No temporal awareness. Used in SD 1.x, SDXL, DALL-E.

Problem for video: No temporal compression. Adjacent frames are encoded independently, causing flicker and temporal inconsistency.

SD 1.5, SDXL, Imagen

3D Spatiotemporal VAE

Compresses video jointly in space and time: T×H×W×3 → T/4×H/8×W/8×16. Temporal features are entangled in the latent — the diffusion model sees a temporally coherent representation.

Key models: MagViT-v2 (Google, tokenizes video into discrete codes), Cosmos Tokenizer (NVIDIA, continuous + discrete), Wan-VAE (Alibaba, unlimited-length), CogVideoX VAE.

All 2024–2025 frontier video models

⚠

Why 2D VAE Fails for Video

If you encode each frame independently with a 2D VAE, the latent codes for adjacent frames have no guaranteed smoothness. The diffusion model must learn temporal coherence entirely on its own, which is extremely difficult. 3D VAEs solve this by compressing the temporal dimension (typically 4×), so the latent space is already temporally smooth. This is why every serious video model post-2023 uses a 3D VAE.

VAE Training Objective

The VAE is trained with a multi-objective loss:

  • L1 reconstruction: Pixel-level fidelity between input and output
  • LPIPS: Perceptual similarity via deep features (VGG)
  • GAN: Adversarial loss from a PatchGAN discriminator for sharpness
  • KL divergence: Regularizes latent distribution toward N(0, I) so the diffusion model can sample from it

The 4×8×8 Consensus

As of 2025, most leading video models converge on 4×8×8 compression (4× temporal, 8× each spatial dimension). This is the sweet spot where temporal detail for smooth motion is preserved while being small enough for efficient diffusion training.

Flow Matching

Flow matching is the training objective that replaced DDPM for frontier video models. Instead of learning to predict and remove noise step-by-step, it learns a continuous velocity field along straight-line paths.

DDPM vs Flow Matching

DDPM (Traditional)

  • Discrete timesteps (T = 1000)
  • Learns noise prediction: εθ(xt, t)
  • Requires noise schedule design
  • ~50–250 sampling steps
  • Curved, stochastic paths

Flow Matching (Modern)

  • Continuous time t ∈ [0, 1]
  • Learns velocity field: vθ(xt, t)
  • Schedule-free (straight paths)
  • ~25–50 sampling steps
  • Straight optimal-transport paths

The Core Idea

Flow matching constructs a time-dependent probability path pt(x) that smoothly interpolates between noise (t=0) and data (t=1). A neural network learns the velocity field vθ that generates this flow.

💡 Intuition: Think of it as transportation

Imagine a fleet of particles starting at random positions (noise) that need to reach specific destinations (data). DDPM gives them wandering, curved routes. Flow matching gives them straight-line highways — the shortest possible paths (optimal transport). Shorter paths = easier to learn = fewer steps to travel.

Reflow: Straightening Trajectories Further

💡 Why Straight Paths Matter

Even with flow matching, the learned velocity field isn't perfectly straight — the model approximates. Reflow (Liu et al., 2023) is a technique to iteratively straighten these paths:

  1. Train a flow matching model normally (pass 1)
  2. Use the trained model to generate (noise, data) pairs by flowing noise to data
  3. Re-train on these paired samples — the new coupling is straighter because the pairs are already matched
  4. Repeat. Each iteration produces straighter paths.

Result: After reflowing, you can sample in as few as 1–4 steps with minimal quality loss (vs. 25–50 steps for first-pass models). This is the theoretical foundation behind distillation techniques used in production (e.g., FLUX-Schnell, InstaFlow).

The DiT Block

adaLN-Zero DiT Block (following Peebles & Xie, 2023)

💡 Key Innovation: adaLN-Zero

Instead of cross-attention for conditioning (like U-Net diffusion), DiT uses Adaptive Layer Normalization. The timestep t and text conditioning c are combined and projected into scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters that modulate the LayerNorm in each block. The "Zero" variant initializes the final linear layer to zero, so each block starts as an identity function — this stabilizes training significantly.

Why Dual/Triple Encoders?

💡 Complementary Strengths

CLIP is trained contrastively on image-text pairs, so it produces embeddings that are naturally aligned between modalities. But its text representation is coarse (77-token limit) and misses compositional details.

T5 is a language model with bidirectional attention, producing rich token-level representations that capture complex relationships and long descriptions. But it has no visual alignment.

Using both gives you: global visual alignment (CLIP) + detailed semantic understanding (T5). Movie Gen goes further with a triple encoder adding character-level precision (ByT5) for accurate text rendering in video.

Separate weight streams per modality but joint attention on concatenated sequences. Most parameter-expensive, strongest text comprehension.

Generation Tasks

Modern video generation systems support a family of related tasks beyond simple text-to-video. Understanding these tasks—and how they relate architecturally—is essential for building a complete system.

Core Generation Tasks

Task Landscape

T2V: Text-to-Video

The foundational task. A text prompt is encoded via T5/CLIP, the DiT denoises from Gaussian noise conditioned on text, and the VAE decodes the latent to pixels. All frontier models support this as the primary task.

I2V: Image-to-Video

Animate a static reference image into a video. Three architectural approaches:

Channel Concat

Encode the first frame through VAE into latent z1. Concatenate channel-wise with noise tensor. DiT input channels expand (e.g., 16 → 32).

SVD, Emu-Video, AnimateAnything

Frame Replacement

Replace the first frame’s noise with the clean latent z1. Input becomes {z1, ε2, ..., εn}. Inject first-frame features into spatial attention K,V.

ConsistI2V, TI2V-Zero

Masking Strategy

Conditioning frames are “unmasked” (timestep=0), frames to generate are noised. Different mask patterns enable I2V, continuation, and interpolation from one model.

Open-Sora, Wan 2.1

V2V: Video-to-Video Editing

Takes an existing video and modifies it. Approaches include DDIM inversion + re-denoising with new conditions, instruction-based editing (Movie Gen’s “backtranslation” approach without supervised editing data), and first-frame editing propagated via I2V (AnyV2V). This remains the least mature task.

T2I: Joint Image-Video Training

💡

Key Insight: Images are 1-frame videos

All frontier models treat images as single-frame videos in the same architecture. This is critical because image-text datasets are ~100× larger than video-text datasets, providing far richer visual concepts. Movie Gen uses a 1:3 image:video batch ratio. Open-Sora VAE trains on 20% images + 80% video.

Other Tasks

Camera Control

Plücker coordinates encode each ray as (o × d, d) ∈ R6. A Camera Encoder processes these into features injected via pixel-wise addition into temporal attention layers. CameraCtrl trains on RealEstate10K (~65K clips).

Video Extension & Interpolation

Auto-regressive chunk generation for longer videos. FIFO-Diffusion uses a queue-based mechanism for infinite-length generation. Open-Sora masks first-K-frames for continuation, first+last frames for interpolation.

Subject-Driven Generation

Inject a reference identity via cross-attention adapters (IP-Adapter style). DreamVideo decouples subject learning from motion learning. Movie Gen adds personalization via post-training on human-centric videos with image reference conditioning.

Video Inpainting / Outpainting

Extend or fill missing regions in spacetime. AVID uses Temporal MultiDiffusion for arbitrary-length inpainting. Open-Sora Plan treats I2V itself as “inpainting in the temporal dimension.”

Dense Captions Are Non-Negotiable

Sora's technical report explicitly credits dense captioning as a key factor in generation quality. Short captions (COCO-style, ~10 words) only describe the main action. Dense captions (~100+ words) describe composition, lighting, camera angle, background, style, and motion — giving the model far richer conditioning signal. Models trained on dense captions follow complex prompts dramatically better and produce more diverse, detailed outputs. This is arguably the single highest-leverage data improvement.

💡 The Captioning Recipe

The emerging best practice: (1) Use a large VLM (70B+) on a random subset for high-quality reference captions, (2) distill into a smaller model (7–13B) that can process the full dataset at scale, (3) produce both short (action-centric) and detailed (visual-descriptive) captions for each clip, (4) include structured dimensions like camera motion, lighting, and style. At inference, short user prompts are expanded to match the detailed training distribution (Sora uses GPT to expand prompts, Movie Gen uses LLaMA).

Specific Filtering Techniques

A

Aesthetic Scoring

A CLIP+MLP predictor trained on human aesthetic ratings. LAION-5B aesthetic classifier is widely used. Typical threshold: score >4.5 (retains ~49% of data). Dover model used by HunyuanVideo combines technical + aesthetic quality in a single score.

B

Motion Filtering

Optical flow estimation to compute per-frame motion magnitude. Remove videos that are too static (slideshows, still images with music) or too jittery (handheld shake, fast random cuts). Open-Sora Plan uses 0.001 ≤ motion score ≤ 0.3. VMAF (Video Multi-method Assessment Fusion) scores used by Movie Gen and Open-Sora 2.0.

C

Scene Detection & Splitting

Raw videos are split into single-shot clips using scene boundary detection. PySceneDetect (HunyuanVideo), TransNetV2 neural network (Cosmos), or HSV histogram similarity (Seaweed). This ensures each training clip is temporally coherent with no hard cuts.

D

Deduplication

Semantic deduplication (SemDeDup): compute CLIP embeddings, k-means cluster into 10K+ groups, remove near-duplicates within each cluster (~30% reduction). Movie Gen additionally uses inverse square root resampling by concept cluster to balance the distribution.

Training at Scale

Training a frontier video generation model requires billions of parameters, thousands of GPUs, and carefully orchestrated multi-stage curricula. The universal recipe: start with images, progressively add video, increase resolution, and finish with quality fine-tuning.

The Universal Training Recipe

Progressive Multi-Stage Training Pipeline
Stage 1: Image Pretraining

Train the full DiT on large text-image datasets. Builds foundational spatial understanding, text-image alignment, and compositional ability. This is the longest and cheapest stage per sample.

↓

The model learns temporal dynamics, motion, and basic physics. Images mixed in to prevent catastrophic forgetting (typical image:video ratio 1:3 to 1:10). Uses the bulk of training compute (25–60% of total steps).

Resolution: 256–360px | FPS: 4–16 | Duration: 2–8s | Data: O(100M) clips + images
↓

Smaller dataset with much stricter quality filtering. Learns high-frequency visual details, fine motion, and longer temporal coherence. Movie Gen requires 60%+ human content at this stage.

Resolution: 480–720p+ | FPS: 16–24 | Duration: 5–16s | Data: O(5–10M) clips
↓

The model predicts v = αtε − σtx0, which balances epsilon-prediction (good at low noise) and x-prediction (good at high noise):

CogVideoX, Open-Sora Plan, SVD

Timestep Sampling

How t is sampled during training significantly affects convergence. Uniform sampling wastes compute on trivial noise levels.

Logit-Normal (SD3/FLUX)

Draw t from a logit-normal distribution with parameters (m=0, s=1). This concentrates training on intermediate timesteps where the model refines perceptually meaningful details. Consistently outperforms uniform sampling.

Used by: Movie Gen, HunyuanVideo, Open-Sora, SD3, FLUX

Min-SNR Weighting

Weight the loss by wt = min(SNR(t), γ) with γ=5. Achieves 3.4× faster convergence than unweighted (ICCV 2023). Treats diffusion training as multi-task learning, balancing conflicts among timesteps.

Used by: Open-Sora Plan (γ=5.0)

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)

During training, text conditioning is randomly dropped (replaced with null embedding) at 10–20% probability. At inference, conditional and unconditional predictions are combined:

Infrastructure & Parallelism

Video diffusion models are among the most memory-hungry models in AI. A single 720p, 5-second video produces ~73K tokens after patchification — far beyond what fits on one GPU. Distributed training isn't optional, it's a requirement.

Parallelism Strategies for Video DiT Training
1

FSDP (Fully-Sharded Data Parallel)

Shards model parameters, gradients, and optimizer states across GPUs. Each GPU holds only 1/N of the model. All-gather before forward, reduce-scatter after backward. Enables 30B+ params to fit in cluster memory. Used by Movie Gen, Open-Sora 2.0.

2

Sequence / Context Parallelism

The video token sequence (73K+ tokens) is split across GPUs along the sequence dimension. Each GPU processes a chunk, with ring-allreduce for cross-chunk attention. Critical for video: image models don't need this, video models can't work without it. HunyuanVideo uses this as part of its 5D parallelism.

3

Tensor Parallelism

Splits individual weight matrices across GPUs (e.g., each GPU handles a slice of the attention heads). Low communication overhead for intra-node (NVLink). Combined with FSDP for inter-node. Movie Gen uses 3D parallelism: FSDP + tensor + sequence.

4

Pipeline Parallelism

Splits model layers across GPUs (stage 1 on GPU-0, stage 2 on GPU-1, etc.). Micro-batching hides the pipeline bubble. Less common for video DiTs but used in the largest deployments. HunyuanVideo's “5D parallelism” = data + tensor + sequence + context + ZeroCache.

Compute Scale

  • Movie Gen (30B): 6,144 H100 GPUs (Meta Grand Teton platform)
  • Cosmos (14B): 10,000 H100s for ~3 months (~22M GPU-hours)
  • HunyuanVideo (13B): 5D parallelism, 99.5% training stability
  • Seaweed-7B: 1,000 H100s, 665K GPU-hours total
  • Open-Sora 2.0 (11B): 224 GPUs, $200K total

Memory Optimization

  • Activation Checkpointing: Save only critical activations, recompute during backward pass. ~12% memory savings.
  • Mixed Precision (BF16): BF16 forward/backward, FP32 master weights. Halves activation memory.
  • Gradient Accumulation: Simulate larger batches without proportional memory cost.
  • Flash Attention: Fused kernels for O(N) memory attention. Essential for 73K+ token sequences.

RL & Alignment

After pre-training, video models are aligned with human preferences using reinforcement learning. This section covers the latest techniques: GRPO, DPO, reward models, and their adaptation to flow matching.

Why Alignment?

Pre-trained video models often produce outputs with artifacts, poor text adherence, or unnatural motion. RL alignment fine-tunes the model to match human preferences across multiple dimensions: visual quality, motion quality, and text faithfulness.

GRPO for Video

💡 From LLMs to Video: How GRPO Adapts

You know GRPO from LLMs: generate a group of responses, rank them by reward, update the policy using relative rankings (no critic needed). For video, the same principle applies but with a key challenge: flow matching models use deterministic ODE paths, so there's no natural exploration mechanism.

The solution: convert the ODE to an SDE by adding controlled noise during sampling. This enables the diversity needed for group-relative ranking while keeping the benefits of flow matching.

DanceGRPO (ByteDance, 2025)

First unified GRPO framework working across both diffusion and rectified flow models, across T2I, T2V, and I2V tasks. Converts ODE to SDE for divergent sampling. Up to 181% improvement on HPS-v2.1.

MixGRPO (Tencent, 2025)

Sliding window approach: SDE sampling only within a window, ODE outside. Reduces training time by ~50% vs DanceGRPO. MixGRPO-Flash reduces by 71%. Used in HunyuanVideo 1.5 production.

Open Problems & Research Frontiers

Despite rapid progress, video generation faces fundamental challenges. These represent the most important research directions for the next 1-3 years.

Current Limitations

⚠ Physics Simulation

Models don't accurately model physical interactions: glass shattering, fluid dynamics, object permanence. Eating food doesn't change object state. Objects float, interpenetrate, or behave non-physically.

T2VPhysBench (2025), World Consistency Score

⚠ Long-Form Consistency

Each generation is typically 4–8 seconds. Longer videos cause drift in character appearance, scene layout, and style. Autoregressive extension introduces compounding errors.

⚠ Fine Detail Control

Complex facial expressions, fine hand movements, and small text remain challenging. Compositional prompts with multiple entities and relationships often fail.

⚠ Audio-Video Sync

Separately generated audio and video inevitably desynchronize. Veo 3 addresses this with joint generation, but it remains unsolved for most models.

⚠ Real-Time Generation

Even optimized inference can't produce high-resolution video in real-time. A 5s 720p video takes hundreds of seconds to generate.

⚠ Reward Hacking

RL alignment reward models can be exploited by the optimizer. Videos may score high on automated metrics while looking worse to humans. Multi-dimensional, robust reward models are needed.

Research Frontiers

Unified RL Frameworks

DanceGRPO and MixGRPO demonstrate single algorithms working across diffusion and flow matching, across T2I/T2V/I2V. Expect further unification and 10× efficiency gains.

Physics-Grounded Generation

World Consistency Score and T2VPhysBench represent early steps. Future models may incorporate learned physics priors or physical simulation engines as reward signals.

Constitutional Video Generation

No equivalent to Constitutional AI exists for video. "Temporal risk" — where individual frames are harmless but sequences are unsafe — is a unique video challenge requiring novel safety approaches.

World Models

As video models evolve toward world simulators (Sora's vision), they must learn physical consistency, causal reasoning, and factual accuracy — not just visual plausibility.

Efficient Alignment

MixGRPO-Flash (71% training time reduction) and ReinFlow (single-step fine-tuning) show RL alignment needn't be prohibitively expensive. Making alignment practical at video scale is an active frontier.

Multi-Objective Pareto Alignment

Optimizing quality vs. safety vs. text faithfulness vs. motion simultaneously rather than a single scalar reward. MJ-VIDEO's MoE and VisionReward's MPO are early approaches.
